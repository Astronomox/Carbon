AKATINS 2026 · PRE-EVENT CHALLENGE
You watched them count.
Then you lost the number.
THE SITUATION
At a polling unit, the counting is public.
Anyone can stand there and watch. The presiding officer counts the ballots out loud, writes the
number on a form, and announces it in front of everyone present. It is the most transparent
moment in the entire process.
Then the number leaves.
Unit to ward. Ward to local government. Local government to state. State to national. At every stop
the numbers are added again, by people you cannot see, in rooms you are not in. And the person
who stood in that compound and watched the count with their own eyes has no way to follow their
number to the end.
They know what they saw. They just can't trace it.

THE CHALLENGE
Design one thing that closes the gap between what someone witnessed and what they are told.
Not a platform. Not a whole system. One thing.
A screen. A way of showing that a number isn't confirmed yet. Twenty seconds explaining how a
result travels. One image of a person standing in a compound watching a number get written down.
Your choice. One thing.
THE HARD PART
A tool that shows unconfirmed numbers with too much confidence is worse than no tool at all.
If your design makes a pending figure look certified, you have built a rumour machine with good
typography. The genuinely difficult problem here is being honest about what isn't known yet, and
making that honesty legible.
That is what we are judging.

WHAT TO SUBMIT
Product Design — One screen or flow. Most likely: what a person sees when they look up their own unit. The hard part is state — confirmed, pending, disputed — and making those read differently at a glance.

Brand Design — A name, a mark, one application. What does trustworthy look like with no institutional backing? It can't look governmental, because that's impersonating authority. It can't look like a startup, because this isn't a product. Solve that.

Motion Design — 15 to 30 seconds. Most people have never seen how a result travels from a unit to a national total. It's a sequence. Show it.

Design Engineering — A working prototype. Real constraint: connectivity at polling units is bad. What does your thing do when the network drops?

Illustrations — One piece. Data always fails at making a number feel like it belongs to a person. Fix that.

The rule. No candidate. No party. No prediction. No real election. This is infrastructure, not commentary. Build the pipe, not the water. Any entry that names a real party, candidate, or election is disqualified.

HOW YOU'RE JUDGED

Craft: Is the execution at a standard that this community respects? — 25%

Honesty: Does your design tell the truth abo what it doesn’t know? — 25%

Originality: Have we seen this before? — 20%
